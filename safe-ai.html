Safe & Responsible AI Use for Manufacturing SMEs

Secure, ethical and human-centric AI adoption for Northwest manufacturers

Source: NCSC, CISA and international partners – Guidelines for Secure AI System Development (November 2023)
Full PDF: Download NCSC Guidelines

Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) systems have the potential to bring many benefits to manufacturing. However, for these opportunities to be fully realised, AI must be developed, deployed and operated in a secure and responsible way.

Cyber security is a necessary precondition for the safety, resilience, privacy, fairness, efficacy and reliability of AI systems.

Why AI Security Is Different

AI systems face novel vulnerabilities known as Adversarial Machine Learning (AML), including:

  • Prompt injection attacks (especially in LLMs)
  • Data poisoning (corrupting training data)
  • Model theft and inversion attacks
  • Supply chain compromises

Secure by Design – Core Principles

  • Principle 1: Take Ownership of Customer Security Outcomes
  • Principle 2: Embrace Radical Transparency and Accountability
  • Principle 3: Lead from the Top – Security must be a board-level priority

The Four-Stage Secure AI Lifecycle

1. Secure Design

Understand threats, conduct risk assessments, apply secure-by-design principles, and choose appropriate models with proper due diligence.

2. Secure Development

Protect training data, document models (Model Cards, SBOMs), manage technical debt, and secure the supply chain.

3. Secure Deployment

Apply strong access controls, segregation, logging, and incident response plans. Release responsibly with clear user guidance.

4. Secure Operation & Maintenance

Monitor inputs and outputs, detect drift and adversarial attacks, automate secure updates, and continuously improve.

Practical Checklist for Manufacturing SMEs

  • Identified the specific business task AI will support
  • Conducted threat modelling and risk assessment
  • Ensured meaningful human oversight for critical decisions
  • Checked GDPR and data privacy compliance
  • Applied secure-by-design and input/output controls
  • Tested for bias, fairness, and adversarial attacks
  • Documented models, data, and limitations
  • Implemented logging, monitoring, and incident response
